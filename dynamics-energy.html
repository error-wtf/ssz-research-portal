Action and conserved quantities

Lagrange–Hamilton formulation

Equatorial metric Lagrangian
ℒ = ½ [−D²c² ṫ² + D⁻² ṙ² + r²φ̇²]

The public ssz-lagrange repository develops an action-based metric treatment, Euler–Lagrange equations, canonical momenta, a Hamiltonian description, geodesic equations, effective potentials, energy-condition calculations and exploratory extensions. Its local regression suite was independently rerun for this portal audit: 54 of 54 checks passed.

Precise scope. It is incorrect to say that no field-equation or action work exists. Public derivations and implementations do exist. A unique, universally accepted covariant fundamental action that closes every static, rotating, cosmological and matter sector is a broader completion question; it is not required to acknowledge the concrete results already present.

For a static diagonal metric, cyclic coordinates generate conserved quantities. The implementation uses those invariants to reduce the trajectory equations and to check numerical conservation. See the dedicated Geodesic Workbench for interactive integration.

Segmented energy

Energy accounting and matter tests

Generic segmented sum
Etotal = Σi Ei

The segmented-energy repository implements N-segment energy accounting, matter profiles and energy-condition diagnostics. Its catalog covers 129 objects; a stricter report isolates 16 objects with complete measured mass-and-radius inputs. These scopes must not be merged into one empirical count.

Weak-energy-condition samples and effective density/pressure quantities have been evaluated in the public code. That makes “energy conditions not evaluated” factually wrong. It does not turn every sampled condition into a proof for all radii, all matter models or every possible inner completion.

Mathematical structure

Radial scaling and spectral checks

The ssz-radial-scaling repository studies logarithmic radial transformations, the transformed time-independent Schrödinger equation, Langer-type corrections and WKB controls. Reported Bohr-spectrum checks for n = 1…5 remain below 0.001% error in the repository’s declared benchmark.

Scope boundary. These mathematical correspondences can validate transformations and numerical methods. They do not by themselves establish a quantum theory of gravity.

Global structure

What “completeness” means here

Public SSZ repositories contain geodesic equations, conserved quantities, finite-domain integrations, action-based derivations, lensing calculations and energy-condition studies. Therefore a blanket “not evaluated” label is misleading.

The remaining question is narrower and more demanding: whether one declared global spacetime, including its areal centre or a replacement inner boundary, admits a maximal causal extension and complete null and timelike geodesics. That requires a specified inner completion and global analysis. It does not erase the existing domain-specific results.
