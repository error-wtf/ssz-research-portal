Formula and
symbol reference

Central equations are stated with units, domains, limits, implementation rules, test links and the misinterpretations they do not permit.

Ξ

Geometry and scale

Schwarzschild scale

rs=2GM/c²

Units: m. Domain: M>0. Example: one solar mass gives about 2.953 km. Code: 2*G*M/c**2. Guardrail: do not use GM/c² under the same symbol.

Diagonal metric

ds²=−D²c²dt²+D⁻²dr²+r²dΩ²

Units: length² per term. Scope: static spherical effective geometry. Test: tensor/inverse/horizon suites. Guardrail: this ansatz is not a derived global solution.

Segment field and branches

Strong branch

Ξ=1−exp(−φr_s/r)

Dimensionless; r/r_s<1.8. Limits: Ξ(r_s)=0.801711847, Ξ→1 inward. Tested by strong-branch and horizon limits.

C² blend

Ξ=H5(t), t=(r/r_s−1.8)/0.4

Endpoint value, slope and curvature come from both branches. A cubic zero-slope smoothstep is not equivalent.

Weak branch

Ξ=r_s/(2r)

Dimensionless; r/r_s>2.2. Limits: 0.05 at 10r_s and zero at infinity.

Clocks, redshift and phase

Time factor

D=1/(1+Ξ), dτ=Ddt

D is dimensionless. At r_s, D≈0.555027709. This demonstrates finite horizon time scaling, not central regularity.

Static redshift proxy

z=1/D−1=Ξ

Dimensionless. Applies to the declared static clock map. Real spectra also require motion, atmosphere, transfer and observer modelling.

Weak-field observable machinery

Lensing

α=(1+γ)r_s/b

Radians; γ=1 in the declared PPN limit. Uses both temporal and spatial metric contributions.

Shapiro delay

Δt=(1+γ)(r_s/c)ln(4r₁r₂/d²)

Seconds. One-way/round-trip geometry is separate from the PPN factor.

Perihelion advance

Δω=6πGM/[a(1−e²)c²]

Radians per orbit in the weak-field β=γ=1 limit. Agreement does not test the SSZ centre.

Interior asymptotics

Metric limit

A=D²→1/4

Finite but not sufficient for local Euclidean/Minkowski regularity at an areal centre.

Ricci scalar

R~3/(2r²)

Unit m⁻². Halving r multiplies the leading divergence by four.

Kretschmann scalar

K~9/(4r⁴)

Unit m⁻⁴. Halving r multiplies the leading divergence by sixteen; it is coordinate-invariant.
