02 · notation and normalisation

The objects are fixed before the argument starts

Use \(\alpha=\xi+i\beta\), with \(\xi\in\mathbb R\), \(0<\beta<\tfrac12\), and \(s=\tfrac12+i\alpha=\tfrac12-\beta+i\xi\). The symbol \(\xi(s)\) denotes the completed zeta function; \(\Xi(\alpha):=\xi(\tfrac12+i\alpha)\) denotes its spectral reparametrisation.

\[ \xi(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(\alpha)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+i\alpha\right). \]

Positive profile

Let \(\theta:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty)\) be the canonical even theta profile in the Zeta repository and set \(\Phi=-\log\theta\). Define \(P=\Phi''\) and \(T=(2\Phi'\Phi''-\Phi''')/\Phi''\).

What is and is not claimed

Certified \(P>0\), \(T\)-margins and matrix residues are internal repository claims. They do not replace independent review of the source transform or the functional-analytic domain assumptions.

03d · the classical Dirichlet series

The familiar definition of \(\zeta(s)\)

For \(\Re s=\sigma>1\), the Riemann zeta function is defined by the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series below. In the critical strip \(0<\sigma<1\), this series is not itself convergent; analytic continuation or the alternating eta relation is required.

Dirichlet series

\[\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^s},\qquad s=\sigma+it,\qquad \sigma>1.\]

Writing \(n^{-s}=n^{-\sigma}e^{-it\log n}\) shows that each term has magnitude \(n^{-\sigma}\) and rotates by the phase \(-t\log n\). Absolute convergence follows from the \(p\)-series \(\sum n^{-\sigma}\).

Finite partial sum and continuation

\[S_N(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{-s},\qquad \eta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-s},\qquad \zeta(s)=\frac{\eta(s)}{1-2^{1-s}}.\]

The live explorer uses finite \(S_N\) or eta samples only as visual approximations. The last identity supplies continuation away from its denominator’s pole; it does not turn a finite plot into a proof of a zero.

Domain warning: at \(\sigma\le1\), do not silently present the raw Dirichlet partial sums as the value of \(\zeta(s)\). The proof candidate uses the completed \(\xi(s)\), the theta transform, and rigorously bounded Volterra tails; this panel explains the classical series and its exact domain.
Animated geometric reading

How the Dirichlet terms walk through the complex plane

Each term \(n^{-s}=n^{-\sigma}e^{-it\log n}\) is a rotating, shrinking vector. The animated gold path joins the partial sums \(S_k(s)=\sum_{n\le k}n^{-s}\); the orange point is the current partial sum and the blue arrow is the newest term.

04 · Volterra solutions

From the positive source to two canonical tails

\[ u_-^\alpha(x)=e^{-i\alpha x}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{i\alpha y}\theta(y)\,dy,\qquad u_+^\alpha(x)=-e^{-i\alpha x}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{i\alpha y}\theta(y)\,dy. \]

Differentiation

Absolute convergence and continuity of \(\theta\) justify differentiating the variable-bound integrals. Both satisfy \(u'_\pm+i\alpha u_\pm=\theta\).

Difference identity

Subtracting the two integrals gives \(u_-^\alpha-u_+^\alpha=e^{-i\alpha x}\int_\mathbb R e^{i\alpha y}\theta(y)dy\). Under the repository’s Xi normalisation this is \(e^{-i\alpha x}\Xi(\alpha)\).

Zero matching: if \(\Xi(\alpha)=0\), the two scalar solutions coincide everywhere; the ODE then also matches their derivatives and the \(F\)-components.

Differentiation written out

For the right tail, (I_+(x)=\int_x^\infty e^{i\alpha y}\theta(y)\,dy) has (I_+'(x)=-e^{i\alpha x}\theta(x)). Differentiating the prefactor and the variable limit gives (u_+'+i\alpha u_+=\theta). For the left tail, (I_-(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x e^{i\alpha y}\theta(y)\,dy) has (I_-'(x)=e^{i\alpha x}\theta(x)), giving the same equation. Absolute convergence makes both identities equalities of finite complex numbers.

Subtracting the two expressions uses the disjoint decomposition (( -\infty,x]\cup[x,\infty)=\mathbb R), so no boundary contribution is omitted.

03a · the analytic starting point

The completed zeta function, its Xi form, and the source transform

The proof candidate starts from the completed zeta function, not from a numerical list of zeros. The completion removes the pole at (s=1), incorporates the gamma factor, and gives the functional equation a symmetric form.

\[\xi(s)=\frac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(\alpha)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+i\alpha\right).\]

Fourier normalisation

The repository fixes the normalisation \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{i\alpha x}\theta(x)\,dx=\Xi(\alpha)\). If a different Fourier convention is used, an explicit nonzero factor must be carried through every matching statement; it may never be silently absorbed.

\[\Xi(-\alpha)=\Xi(\alpha),\qquad \Xi(\alpha)=0\Longleftrightarrow \xi\!\left(\tfrac12+i\alpha\right)=0.\]

Positive source

Set \(\theta(x)>0\) and \(\Phi(x)=-\log\theta(x)\). The canonical profile is even, so \(\Phi\) is even, \(\Phi'\) is odd and \(\Phi''\) is even. The certified bounds prove \(\Phi''>0\) and the required \(T\)-margins on the compact and far domains.

\[P=\Phi'',\qquad T=\frac{2\Phi'\Phi''-\Phi'''}{\Phi''}.\]

Why the source is integrable

For (x\ge0), the positive Gaussian representation gives a bound of the form \(\theta(x)\le C_\theta e^{-(2\pi-9/2)x}\). Evenness then yields \(e^{|\beta||x|}\theta(x)\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) whenever \(|\beta|<1/2\), because \(2\pi-9/2-|\beta|>0\). This is the analytic fact that turns both Volterra expressions into actual functions.

\[\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{i\alpha x}\theta(x)|\,dx\le\frac{2C_\theta}{2\pi-\frac92-|\beta|}<\infty.\]

05 · matrix identity

The certified scalars must be the actual Hermitian residues

A

Right system

With \(F=-i(u'+\Phi'u)\) and \(Y=(u,F)^\mathsf T\), the exported system is \(Y'=A_\alpha Y\), \(A_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}-\Phi'&i\\-i\Phi''&-i\alpha\end{pmatrix}\).

\[H_+=J_+'+A_\alpha^*J_++J_+A_\alpha= e^{2\Phi-2\beta x}\operatorname{diag}\!\left(2\beta,\frac{2\Phi'\Phi''-\Phi'''}{(\Phi'')^2}\right).\]
B

Reflected left system

For \(t=-x\) and \(Z=P_0Y(-t)\), \(P_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), parity gives \(A_- =\begin{pmatrix}-p&i\\-iq&i\alpha\end{pmatrix}\), where \(p=\Phi'\), \(q=\Phi''\).

\[H_-=e^{2\Phi-2\beta t}\begin{pmatrix}2\beta&ik_\beta\\-ik_\beta&\frac{(1+k_\beta)(T-4\beta)+k_\beta'}{\Phi''}\end{pmatrix}.\]
C

Schur certificate

The upper-left entry is \(2\beta>0\). The Schur complement is \(e^{2\Phi-2\beta t}G_\beta/\Phi''\), with \(G_\beta=(1+k_\beta)(T-4\beta)+k_\beta'-\Phi''k_\beta^2/(2\beta)\). The compact, far-field and Sturm certificates prove the required strict inequalities in their declared domains.

Why this is the actual Lyapunov residue

The derivative of \(Y^*JY\) contains the exported system matrix \(A_\alpha\), not an independent scalar surrogate. Reflection changes the lower-right entry from \(-i\alpha\) to \(+i\alpha\), producing the \(T-4\beta\) term. The correction creates the off-diagonal \(ik_\beta\) terms. Therefore the certified \(G_\beta\) is exactly the Schur complement of the matrix used in the Green identity.

06 · endpoint decay

Why the boundary flux vanishes

For \(m=8-B_{DR}>\tfrac12\), \(p_0=\inf_{x\ge1/2}\Phi''(x)>0\), fixed \(0<\beta<\tfrac12\), and finite \(|\alpha|\), define

\[C_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{(m-\beta)^2}+\frac{1}{p_0}\left(1+\frac{m}{m-\beta}+\frac{|\alpha|}{m-\beta}\right)^2.\]

Convexity of \(\Phi(y)\pm\beta y\) gives the tail estimate \(\int_R^\infty e^{-\psi(y)}dy\le e^{-\psi(R)}/\psi'(R)\). Thus \(|u_\pm|\le\theta(R)/(m-\beta)\), \(|F_\pm|\le C_F\theta(R)\), and \(|M_\pm(\pm R)|\le C_{\alpha,\beta}e^{-2\beta R}\to0\).

Quantifier discipline: the constant may depend on the particular finite \(|\alpha|\); no uniform bound in \(\xi=\Re\alpha\) is claimed, and β=0 is not covered.

Convex-tail lemma

If \(\psi'>0\) and \(\psi''\ge0\) on \([R,\infty)\), convexity gives \(\psi(y)\ge\psi(R)+\psi'(R)(y-R)\). Integrating the exponential majorant gives \(\int_R^\infty e^{-\psi(y)}dy\le e^{-\psi(R)}/\psi'(R)\). Apply this to \(\psi_+=\Phi+\beta x\) and \(\psi_-=\Phi-\beta x\). The certified lower bound \(m>1/2\) makes both denominators positive for every fixed \(0<\beta<1/2\). Since \(\theta=e^{-\Phi}\), substitution into the flux matrix cancels \(e^{2\Phi}\theta^2=1\), leaving only \(e^{-2\beta R}\).

07 · global balance

Finite Green identities become improper identities

Finite intervals

\[M_-(0)-M_-(-R)=E_-(R),\qquad M_+(R)-M_+(0)=E_+(R).\]

These follow from \(\frac d{dx}(Y^*JY)=Y^*(J'+A^*J+JA)Y\), with the left and right outward orientations fixed before taking limits.

Limit

Endpoint decay gives \(E_-=\lim E_-(R)=M_-(0)\) and \(E_+=\lim E_+(R)=-M_+(0)\). Therefore:

\[M_-(0)-M_+(0)=E_-+E_+.\]

09 · classical translation

From the candidate’s α-strip to RH

\[\Xi(-\alpha)=\xi\!\left(\frac12-i\alpha\right)=\xi\!\left(1-\left(\frac12+i\alpha\right)\right)=\xi\!\left(\frac12+i\alpha\right)=\Xi(\alpha).\]

The contradiction excludes \(0<\Im\alpha<\tfrac12\), which is \(0<\Re s<\tfrac12\). Evenness excludes the reflected half \(-\tfrac12<\Im\alpha<0\). The line \(\Im\alpha=0\) remains exactly \(\Re s=\tfrac12\).

Nontrivial zeros

The completed \(\xi(s)\) is entire and its zeros correspond to nontrivial zeta zeros; the pole and trivial zeros are treated by the completion and lie outside the open critical strip.

What is excluded

Only the two open halves are targeted. No argument here excludes the critical line, and no endpoint-decay estimate is silently extended to β=0.
