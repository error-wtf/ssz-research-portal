What the demo actually computes

A small Hamiltonian, not a universal solution

The accompanying script discretises one radial coordinate, chooses illustrative dimensionless parameters, and asks a tridiagonal eigensolver for the lowest numerical modes.

Toy potential

SSZ-inspired damping

\[\Xi(r)=e^{-r_s/r},\qquad D(r)=1-\Xi(r),\qquad V(r)=-\frac{D(r)}{r}\]

This is an effective potential ansatz for the demo, not a derived quantum field theory.

Finite-difference operator

One-dimensional box

\[H\psi=E\psi,\qquad H=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}+V(r)\]

The grid starts at a small positive \(r_{\min}\), so the origin is not solved exactly.

Honest interpretation

“Solved” means numerically diagonalised

\[\psi(r,t)=\psi_n(r)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}\]

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of this matrix are computed; no claim is made about all quantum systems, spectra, or gravity.

What is in the repository

Finite differences + tridiagonal eigensolver

The archived mass-projection repository and its linked calculation-suite Easter egg describe a unit-mass, dimensionless demonstration with \(r_{\min}>0\), a finite box, and standard numerical linear algebra. Negative energies are labelled bound-like within that box; positive modes are box-continuum artefacts.

Why this is a wink, not evidence

Scientific status: this tab is a humorous educational add-on. It does not validate SSZ, solve quantum mechanics, establish bound states in nature, or demonstrate a quantum-gravity coupling. Any serious claim would require a complete model, units, boundary conditions, uncertainty analysis, physical data, independent reproduction and peer review.

The portal documents the experiment because omitting the fun would be dishonest. The cat is allowed to remain in superposition between “cute” and “please submit a methods appendix.” 🐈
